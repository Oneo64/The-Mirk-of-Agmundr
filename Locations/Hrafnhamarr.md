# Hrafnhamarr
Hrafnhamarr is a coastal village in eastern Newfoundland. It was established some time around AD 902 as a small farmstead by the Viking explorer Agmundr. He was unfortunately shot and killed by the natives after a dispute, passing the chieftainship to his son, Hallgrímr Agmundsson, who was still in Iceland. In AD 922, Hallgrímr took approximately 50 settlers to Hrafnhamarr.

There are several buildings in Hrafnhamarr, all made of pine wood and bearing turf roofs. Amongst them is a large hall for community gatherings, and another hall for Hallgrímr's family. Farmlands are cultivated to grow mainly barley. The coast itself is a rocky bay with hills inland, surrounded by a dark pine forest. A river flows downhill from the forest to the ocean, marking the boundary between the village and a grassy meadow.

In AD 923, Ingólfr seized Hrafnhamarr and captured Hallgrímr with the help of native Newfoundland trolls. After the second attempt, he succeeded in capturing the chieftain and had the intent of beheading him. Fortunately, his army was ambushed by three warriors, who were returning from a hunt. Alfarr, one of the warriors, then stabbed Ingólfr to death after knocking him down with a shield charge, ending the usurpation.

## Etymology
The name Hrafnhamarr comes from [hrafn](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/hrafn#Old_Norse) *("raven")* + [hamarr](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/hamarr#Old_Norse) *("crag")*.
