# Hrafnhamarr
Hrafnhamarr is a coastal village in eastern Newfoundland. It was established some time around AD 902 as a small farmstead by the Viking explorer Agmundr. The title of chieftain has been passed to his son, Hallgrímr Agmundsson.

The coast of Hrafnhamarr is a rocky bay with hills inland, surrounded by a dark pine forest. A river flows downhill from the forest to the ocean, marking the boundary between the village and a grassy meadow.

In AD 922, Ingólfr seized Hrafnhamarr and captured Hallgrímr with the help of native Newfoundland trolls. After the second attempt, he succeeded in capturing the chieftain and had the intent of beheading him. Fortunately, his army was ambushed by three warriors, who were returning from a hunt. Alfarr, one of the warriors, then stabbed Ingólfr to death after knocking him down with a shield charge, ending the usurpation.

## Etymology
The name Hrafnhamarr comes from [hrafn](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/hrafn#Old_Norse) *("raven")* + [hamarr](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/hamarr#Old_Norse) *("crag")*.
