# Awakening in the Forest
Ljorn saw Stafnir stop before him. He was half-expecting him to pull out a scroll and read from it, but the messenger simply opened his mouth to deliver his message.

"Sire," Stafnir said, panting. "Hallgrímr has requested both of you."

"Why?" Ljorn asked. "Where is he?"

"He is inside the hall," Stafnir said quickly. "Community hall. He's organising a group to go into the forest."

At the messenger's words, Ljorn got a gut feeling that it wouldn't end well. Even though he'd take peaceful walks in the forest, he always avoided getting near the deeper, darker parts of the forest; he was afraid of them.

"There is another I must inform, so good luck." Hurriedly, the messenger returned to the settlement.

Alfarr looked excited. He stuffed his axe back into his belt and pointed at the largest building in the settlement. "Ljorn, this is our chance!" he exclaimed.

"What are you hoping to find in that forest?" Ljorn said. But Alfarr was already moving towards the community hall. "I..." Stunned, he watched Alfarr as he disappeared into the houses, before following him.

Pushing through the doors of the hall, Ljorn found Hallgrímr next to his daughter, Sága, who proudly held her helmet. In the corner, Ljorn spotted a cloaked man standing close by. Alfarr was putting on a hauberk.

"If you find anything strange, don't mess with it. Something might—" They stopped talking. Hallgrímr spoke up as he welcomed Ljorn. "You're here now! We're now waiting for another; she should be here in a moment."

The cloaked man in the corner stepped forward to get a better look at Ljorn. He was one whom the blond man had never seen nor met. He was boldly dressed in a bear-hide cloak, displaying his ferocity. His hair was long and dark. Perhaps it was the way he held himself, but Ljorn didn't like him.

"My chieftain," Ljorn said to grab Hallgrímr's attention. "I... I am just a farmer, why did you choose me?"

Ljorn had a bad night. After rowing for an hour straight in the rain, all those forest sounds kept him awake. Now, he and Alfarr were felling a tall pine tree for the new longhouse.

The two brothers looked similar. Ljorn was blond and had wavy hair, although it looked like dried wheat. His blue eyes were angled downwards and had a hood, which made him look about 30 years older than he was.

However, Alfarr was more vibrant. His hair was rich and brown, and his eyes were brighter blue and looked young and bright. To Ljorn, he never looked intimidating.

"It is falling!" Ljorn shouted out, warning anyone nearby to watch out. He struck it again. The tree wobbled and bent, and with a rustling crash, fell to the ground. They surrounded the crown of the tree, cleaving the branches off and tossing them to the side. Once the tree was bare, they lifted the trunk and carried it back to the farmstead.

"Excellent," said the overseer as they lowered the tree log next to the scaffolding. "We need ten more of these. Keep moving!"

Ljorn could feel his exhaustion catching up to him. He shivered and shook his arms, relieving the burn in his arms. Exhausted, he began to make his way to the fence. He looked at the ground for a place to sit, but remembered that the overseer is watching him.

Cursing under his breath, Ljorn made his way back to the treeline with Alfarr. He began hacking at the stump while his brother worked on another tree. Eventually, he couldn't work more and slumped against his tree. "I need some time," he tiredly exhaled.

"Whatever suits you," Alfarr said without stopping.

Ljorn put his axe onto the ground and rested for a moment. Just a quick rest, he said to himself. He could feel himself getting drowsy just sitting there.

"Alright," Ljorn muttered as he pulled himself up. He took his axe and went right back to work.

Just then, someone rushed towards the two woodsmen. Ljorn heard the footsteps and spun around, frightened for some reason. It was Stafnir, the messenger.

Ljorn was half-expecting him to pull out a scroll and read from it, but the messenger simply opened his mouth to deliver his message.

"Sire," Stafnir said, panting after the run. "Hallgrímr has requested both of you."

"Where is he?" Alfarr asked.

"He is inside the hall," Stafnir said quickly. "Community hall. He's organising a group to go into the forest."

"The... the forest?" Ljorn repeated. At the messenger's words, he felt something unpleasant in his gut that it wouldn't end well. Even though he'd take peaceful walks in the forest, he always avoided getting near the deeper, darker parts of the forest; he was afraid of them.

"There is another I must inform, so good luck." Hurriedly, the messenger returned to the settlement.

Alfarr looked excited. He stuffed his axe back into his belt and pointed at the largest building in the settlement. "This is our chance!" he exclaimed.

Ljorn, however, didn't look happy at all. He turned to Alfarr and gave him a grim look. "What are you hoping to find in that forest?"

But Alfarr was already moving towards the community hall. "I..." Stunned, he watched Alfarr as he disappeared into the houses, before following him.

As Ljorn passed by the construction site, the overseer shouted out at him. "Hey! Where do you think you two are going?"

"We must see Hallgrímr," Ljorn said, before catching up with Alfarr.

Pushing through the doors of the hall, Ljorn found Hallgrímr next to his daughter, Sága, who proudly held her helmet. In the corner, Ljorn spotted a cloaked man standing close by.

"If you find anything strange, don't mess with it. Something might—" They stopped talking. Hallgrímr spoke up as he welcomed the two men. "You're here now! We're now waiting for another; she should be here in a moment."

The cloaked man in the corner stepped forward to get a better look at them. He was one whom Ljorn had never seen nor met. He was boldly dressed in a bear-hide cloak, displaying his ferocity. His hair was long and dark. Perhaps it was the way he held himself, but Ljorn didn't like him.

"My chieftain," Ljorn said. He was still unsure about the forest mission. "I... I am just a farmer, why did you choose me?"

"You are not a farmer," Hallgrímr said with confidence. "You came with weapons and armour. Don't tell me you are a farmer. Well? Go don them now!"

Ljorn bowed his head. He turned to Alfarr and joined him in donning armour. He pulled out his maille shirt from his kist and slid into it, then took his helmet. Admittingly, wearing his armour again made him feel safe. He shifted to get comfortable in it.

Alfarr, however, struggled to get into his hauberk. He looked like a fish swimming against the river current.

"Do you need help?" Ljorn asked as he slung his sword baldric over his shoulder and placed his helmet over his head.

"Yes."

Ljorn got behind Alfarr and held his hauberk as Alfarr wriggled his way into the armour. Once set, they both returned to the group just in time for the hall doors to open again.

Someone came in and closed the door, careful not to make a bang, and walked towards them with an unassuming stride. She had wavy brown hair, green almond eyes, and a splatter of freckles across her face, and wore a blue dress with a frayed cloak.

The woman gave a quiet greeting and didn’t say anything else.
